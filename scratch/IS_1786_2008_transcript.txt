FOREWORD (extract)
This Indian Standard (Fourth Revision) was adopted by the Bureau of Indian Standards after the draft
finalized by the Concrete Reinforcement Sectional Committee had been approved by the Civil Engineering
Division Council. First published 1961; revised 1966, 1979, 1985, and 2008 (fourth revision).
Important modifications in this revision:
 a) A new strength grade Fe 600 has been introduced.
 b) Two categories based on elongation for each grade except Fe 600 have been introduced.
 c) A new parameter 'percentage total elongation at maximum force' has been introduced.
 d) Nominal sizes have been rationalized and nominal sizes 7 mm, 18 mm, 22 mm, 45 mm and 50 mm have
    been removed (NOTE: Amendment No. 1 later re-introduced 45 mm and 50 mm — see Cl 6.1 below).
Test methods correspond to ISO: IS 1608 (ISO 6892) Tensile testing; IS 1599 (ISO 7438/15630-1) Bend
test; IS 1786 re-bend (ISO 15630-1).

1 SCOPE
1.1 (Substituted by Amendment No. 1) This standard covers the requirements of deformed steel bars and
wires for use as reinforcement in concrete, in the following strength grades:
 a) Fe 415, Fe 415D, Fe 415S;
 b) Fe 500, Fe 500D, Fe 500S;
 c) Fe 550, Fe 550D; and
 d) Fe 600.
NOTES
 1 The figures following the symbol Fe indicate the specified minimum 0.2 percent proof stress or yield
   stress, in N/mm2.
 2 The letters D and S following the strength grade indicates the categories with same specified minimum
   0.2 percent proof stress/yield stress but with enhanced and additional requirements.

GRADE ENUMERATION (option list, verbatim): Grades are Fe 415, Fe 415D, Fe 415S, Fe 500, Fe 500D,
Fe 500S, Fe 550, Fe 550D, Fe 600.

1.2 This standard allows the chemical composition and carbon equivalent to be limited so that the
material can be readily welded by conventional welding procedures.
1.3 Applies to hot-rolled steel without subsequent treatment, hot-rolled steel with controlled cooling
and tempering, and cold-worked steel. Production process at discretion of manufacturer.
1.4 Applies to bars/wires supplied in coil form but requirements apply to the straightened product.
1.5 Also applies to reinforcing bars/wires which may be subsequently coated.
1.6 Deformed bars produced by re-rolling finished products such as plates and rails or material for
which the metallurgical history is not documented/known are not acceptable.

2 REFERENCES: IS 228 (Parts 1 to 24) chemical analysis of steels; IS 1387:1993; IS 1599:1985 Bend test;
IS 1608:2005 Tensile testing; IS 2062:2006; IS 2770 (Part 1):1967 Pull-out test; IS 9417:1989 welding;
IS 11587:1986 weather resistant steels.

NOTES to 4.2:
 1 For guaranteed weldability, Carbon Equivalent CE = C + Mn/6 + (Cr+Mo+V)/5 + (Ni+Cu)/15 shall not be
   more than 0.53 percent when micro-alloy/low alloys are used. When micro-alloys/low alloys are not used,

   0.30 percent total.

5 REQUIREMENTS FOR BOND
5.1 High strength deformed bars/wires shall satisfy requirements in either 5.2 or 5.7 for routine
testing. Pull-out test per 5.7 shall be done in addition to 5.2 for approval of new/amended geometry.
5.2 Deformations and Surface Characteristics. For high strength deformed bars/wires, the mean area of
ribs (mm2) per unit length (mm) above the core, projected on a plane normal to the axis, calculated per
5.4 shall not be less than the following values:
 a) 0.12 phi for phi <= 10 mm,
 b) 0.15 phi for 10 mm < phi <= 16 mm, and
 c) 0.17 phi for phi > 16 mm,
 where phi is the nominal diameter of bar/wire, in mm.
The mean projected area of transverse ribs alone shall be not less than one-third of the values above.
5.3, 5.4, 5.5, 5.6: rib geometry definitions and measurement.
5.7 When subjected to pull-out testing in accordance with IS 2770 (Part 1), the bond strength calculated
from the load at a measured slip of 0.025 mm and 0.25 mm for deformed bars/wires shall exceed that of a
plain round bar of the same nominal size by 40 percent and 80 percent respectively.

9 TESTS
9.1 Selection and Preparation of Test Sample (IS 2062 applies). 9.1.1 test pieces selected by purchaser.
9.2 Tensile Test. Tensile strength, percentage elongation, percentage total elongation, and 0.2 percent
proof stress per IS 1608 with IS 2062. 9.2.1 proof stress at total strain 0.4% (Fe 415/415D), 0.45%
(Fe 500/500D/500S), 0.47% (Fe 550/550D/Fe 600). 9.2.2 stresses calculated using effective cross-section.

9.3 Bend Test. Performed per IS 1599; the maximum mandrel diameter for different grades as specified in
Table 4. The test piece bent cold doubled over the mandrel until the sides are parallel. Passed if no
rupture or cracks visible to a person of normal or corrected vision on the bent portion.

9.4 Rebend Test. Test piece bent to included angle of 135 deg using a mandrel of appropriate diameter
(see 9.4.1), aged in boiling water (100 C) for 30 min and allowed to cool, then bent back to included
angle of 157.5 deg. Passed if no rupture or cracks visible on the rebent portion.

9.5 Retest. Should any test piece fail, two further samples selected; if both pass, material deemed to
comply; if either fails, material does not comply.
